## Devlog de itch (y último post en r/incremental_games, martes 8/09)

**Título**: Version 1.0 — the game has an ending now, and I'm calling it finished

> Pipes and Life is finished. Not abandoned — finished, which is a different
> thing and a rarer one.
>
> **What's new in 1.0:** the game has an ENDING. When all 36 villages drink
> from your network, the camera pulls back over the whole district and the
> celebration runs village by village in the order they joined; then a card
> with your numbers — residents served, tiles of network, years in the
> trade — and Manuel, the old foreman, says the only thing he can. Nothing is
> erased: keep managing, or relocate with a seniority bonus for a completed
> district. It came straight from the plusone 2025 player survey: "the game
> has an ending" was the most wanted feature in the genre, and this game
> tended to infinity without a finish line.
>
> Also in 1.0, from the last two weeks: the click is gone (your first pump
> retires your finger), water visibly flows through every connected pipe, a
> garbage truck drives your waste routes, the bottleneck marks itself, springs
> surface as you explore, and renewals carry a real fixed works cost.
>
> **Why finished:** I'm a water supply professional, not a game developer,
> and I built this to show how tap water actually happens. It does that now.
> I've learned more in these months than in any course — about games, about
> tools, and about how much of my own trade fits in a browser tab. But I
> don't play incremental games myself, and a game its author doesn't play
> shouldn't keep growing out of duty. So it stays here, complete, free, in
> English and Spanish, and I'll answer anyone who writes.
>
> What's next: a different kind of game, for schools in my district — a
> drop of water travelling the whole cycle, from the cloud to the tap and
> back to the river, through our real facilities. If that one works, it's
> because of everything this one taught me.
>
> Thanks to everyone who played, and especially to the people here who
> told me what was confusing. Every one of those comments is in the game.
>
