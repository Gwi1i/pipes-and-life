# El post propio: "You said the click was tedious. It's gone."

La regla 3 de r/incremental_games permite UN post sobre tu juego cada 30
días, aparte del hilo del viernes. Este es el primero que usamos, y va con
el cambio más grande desde el lanzamiento: el clic se jubila. Momento
recomendado: **martes o miércoles por la tarde (hora europea)** — entre
semana hay más gente que el fin de semana, y así el sinclic lleva unos
días publicado y el contador ya tiene una primera cohorte.

**Título del post**:

> Pipes and Life — you said the click was tedious. It's gone: your first pumping station now retires your finger

**Primer comentario** (pegar nada más publicar):

> Two weeks ago I posted my water-utility incremental here for the first time. The most common feedback: the pump click felt like a chore. Fair. So the click is gone — almost.
>
> **How it works now:** at the very start you pump by hand (click the town, or space). It's deliberately a bit of a slog. The moment you connect your first pumping station, it takes over — 4 strokes per second, forever — and the game throws a little party for it. Your finger is retired. From then on every click is a decision: where the pipe goes, what to renew, what to explore. The cold playtesters called the handover "the best moment of the run", which is exactly what the slog before it is for.
>
> **Also since last time:** you can see the water flowing in every connected pipe (a dry pipe = a disconnected pipe), a garbage truck drives your waste routes, the network bottleneck marks itself in red, mountain springs surface as you explore (the one groundwater clue that never lies), and renewing a pipeline now carries a fixed works cost — mobilising the crew costs the same for 5 tiles as for 50, which is how it actually works.
>
> Free, no ads, in your browser: https://gwiii.itch.io/pipes-and-life (also on Newgrounds). English and Spanish.
>
> About me: I work in water supply for a rural association in Navarre, Spain. Everything in the game — why a hamlet is the hardest place to serve, why two wells on one aquifer yield the same as one, why you never renew half a pipeline — is the real thing. If you finish a session knowing something about your own tap water, the game did its job.
>
> AI disclosure: built with an AI assistant (Claude) that wrote the code under my direction; card illustrations, voice and music are AI-generated. The design, the water logic and every decision about what the game says are mine.
>
> What I'd love to hear: does the handover moment land for you? And is the hand-pumping stretch before it too long, too short, or about right?
